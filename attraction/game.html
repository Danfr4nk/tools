measuring…

Map what you find attractive. Numerically.

Iterative A/B testing of synthetic faces. Broad archetypes first, then single-variable pairs. Every face is measured in-browser with facial landmarks — ratios, not adjectives.

Rules

  • Eye color is pinned. Every face in the bank is brown-eyed. It is never the deciding variable.
  • Phase 1 — pick/rank 1 of 4 structural archetypes: wide-angular, long-narrow, heart, round.
  • Phase 2 (adaptive) — the engine drills the highest-uncertainty micro-variable next: jaw, lips, eye spacing, brows, nose. Every pick is scored on measured metric deltas, z-scored against the bank. Optional per-feature A/B calls are direct evidence: they override the holistic read for their metric, count toward any axis whose target they touch, and confounded holistic reads with no direct backup are dropped. Axes retire at 3 consistent or 6 inconclusive evidence points. The play HUD shows live evidence dots and why the queue picked the current pair.
  • Rank mode: click faces in order of preference, then lock the ranking. Richer than single-pick.
  • Feature picks (phase 2): for each measured difference between the pair, optionally pick which face's version you prefer — "A's jaw, B's eyes" becomes data, not just one holistic vote. Tap again to clear. Ø = you genuinely can't tell them apart — that's discrimination data, not a skip. Untouched rows are just skipped: missing, not evidence.
  • Confidence: 1 pick = weak · 2 consistent = leaning · 3+ = confirmed.
